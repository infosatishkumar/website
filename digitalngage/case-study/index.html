The challenge

A capable agency with a template-looking site.

DigitalNgage runs social media, ads, SEO and websites for its clients — and also builds e-commerce stores and custom CRM software. None of that showed on its own website.

One page pretending to be five

All "pages" lived in one index.html and were shown or hidden with JavaScript. Every section shared one URL, so nothing could be shared or found on Google on its own.

No proof of work

Only six services in short cards, and no case studies — even though the team had built the KIO Organics store and the Metro CRM.

A contact form that misbehaved

Stray characters after the closing PHP tag meant the form's reply was not valid JSON, and the email's Reply-To line took the visitor's input without checking it.

Didn't feel like a top agency

Static layout, emoji icons and no motion — the site didn't match the quality of the work.

"Latest, dynamic, fully animated — animation in every section. It should feel like a top digital marketing agency, with every page a marketing website needs."— DigitalNgage, in the project brief

Home page

A home page that tells the whole story.

From the first headline to the footer, every section answers one question a prospect has: what do you do, who for, how, and why you.

01 — HERO

A headline that keeps moving

"We make brands go viral / sell online / rank #1 / get leads / scale fast digitally." The rotating phrase sits over an interactive network of connected dots — a nod to the graph line in the DN logo — that moves away from the cursor. Floating cards show real numbers, and a ticker lists every service.

02 — SERVICES

Eleven services at a glance

Each card has its own icon, a short promise and tags. A soft highlight follows the cursor, the icon tilts on hover, and the whole card links to that service's section on the Services page.

03 — CASE STUDIES

Proof, stacked like cards

KIO Organics and Metro CRM appear as large cards that stick and stack as you scroll; the one underneath gently shrinks back. Each shows three hard numbers — 8 payment options, 6,000+ leads, 27,000+ follow-up notes.

04 — PROCESS

Five steps, scrolled sideways

The section pins in place and the five steps — audit, strategy, create, launch, scale — slide past horizontally while you scroll down, with a progress line underneath. On phones it becomes a simple vertical list.

05 — WHY US

A bento grid of reasons

Marketing plus technology in one team, 360° coverage, data-first reporting, 24/7 monitoring, no lock-ins and a dedicated account manager — with bars that grow as the section comes into view.

06 — TRUST & FOOTER

Testimonials, FAQs and a big sign-off

An auto-playing testimonial slider, six FAQs that open one at a time, a call-to-action block and a footer with every service and case study, ending in a giant DigitalNgage wordmark.

Motion design

Animation in every section — without getting in the way.

Motion is built with GSAP and ScrollTrigger, with Lenis for smooth scrolling. All three libraries are bundled with the site, so nothing depends on an outside CDN.

Word-by-word headlines

Every heading rises into place word by word as it enters the screen.

Interactive network

A canvas of connected dots in the hero that drifts and moves away from the cursor. It pauses when off screen.

Magnetic buttons & cursor

Buttons lean towards the pointer; a custom cursor grows over links and shows "View" or "Read" on cards.

Counting numbers

Stats count up from zero when they scroll into view, formatted the Indian way (6,000+).

Sticky stacking cards

Case study cards pin and stack; images move slightly slower than the page for depth.

Pinned sideways scroll

The process section scrolls horizontally; marquees lean with the speed of your scroll.

Respectful by default: a short loading screen shows only on the first visit, pages open with a single animation, and visitors who turn on "reduce motion" on their device get a calm, fully static version of every page. If JavaScript is off, all content is still visible.

Inner pages

Every page a prospect looks for.

Services, Work, About, Packages, Insights and Contact — plus two full case studies and a custom 404 page.

On mobile

Designed for the phone first.

Every page was checked at phone width with no sideways scrolling. A full-screen menu opens from the corner, and a WhatsApp button is always one tap away.

Working with the client

Shaped by real feedback.

After the first version went live, the client reviewed it on their own screen. Each round of feedback was turned around and re-checked on desktop and mobile.

Colour

"The gradient background doesn't look good — don't use gradients."

Replaced every gradient and background glow with solid brand orange, amber and violet on solid ink and paper.

Layout

"The website should be full screen — there's a gap on the left and right."

Removed the fixed-width column so content spans the full screen, with a small comfortable margin.

Transitions

"Opening a page plays the animation twice — it should happen once."

Removed the page-change curtain; each page now opens with one entrance animation.

Services

"The service buttons should be on the left, not the top."

Moved the 12 services into a sticky left sidebar with numbers and a consultation button.

Articles

"Articles look like mobile view on desktop."

Built a three-column reading layout: contents list, wider article and a sidebar with a CTA and related posts.

Favicon

"Use the DN logo as the favicon."

Turned the DN mark into a transparent favicon in every size, plus a home-screen icon.

Under the hood

Simple to host, simple to update.

The client hosts on standard shared hosting, so the site is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with one small PHP file — no Node server, no monthly platform fee.

One header, thirteen pages

A tiny Python build script stitches shared parts into every page, so the menu and footer are always identical.

  • Pages live in src/pages; shared head, header and footer in src/partials
  • Each page sets its own title, description and active menu item
  • The build also writes the sitemap
  • Upload the public_html folder and the site is live

A lead form that holds up

The PHP form handler was rewritten to be strict about what it accepts.

  • Always returns clean JSON, so the page can show success or a fallback
  • Line breaks stripped from single-line fields to block email header injection
  • Phone and email checked on the server; a hidden field quietly drops spam bots
  • If sending fails, visitors are offered call and WhatsApp instead

Found on Google

  • A real URL, title and description for every page
  • Open Graph images for WhatsApp, LinkedIn and Facebook previews
  • Business details as structured data, plus sitemap and robots files
  • Article headings have links, so sections can be shared directly

Fast and accessible

  • Images load only when they come near the screen
  • Compression and browser caching set in .htaccess
  • Skip link, visible keyboard focus and labelled buttons
  • Full support for the "reduce motion" setting
